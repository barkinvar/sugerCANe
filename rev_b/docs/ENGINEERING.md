# Rev B design decisions and limits

**Scope: Rev B.** [Revision overview](https://github.com/barkinvar/candi-clone/blob/main/rev_b/README.md) · [All revisions](https://github.com/barkinvar/candi-clone/blob/main/REVISIONS.md).

The 60 × 68 mm outline occupies 4,080 mm², versus 9,216 mm² for Rev A: 55.7% less area. Three sensor ports preserve useful capacity while leaving space for push-in terminals, consistent selector placement and an accessible programming cable. Four 3.2 mm mounting holes are centered at (3.5,3.5), (56.5,3.5), (3.5,64.5), and (56.5,64.5) mm. Use insulated mounting hardware and confirm the selected washer/enclosure clearances. The terminal housings extend approximately 8.5 mm above the PCB; the 3.5 mm leads project below the top seating plane.

The design retains a STM32F042K6T6 in LQFP32 and a separate TCAN3413 CAN transceiver. An ESP32-C3 has a CAN/TWAI controller, not an integrated physical CAN transceiver. Retaining the small bare-metal STM32 implementation avoids introducing a radio/module, new firmware dependencies and another untested CAN implementation during the mechanical redesign. This is an original Team Use protocol, not CTRE compatibility.

The input path uses a fuse, reverse-polarity MOSFET and 22 V standoff TVS. The input bulk capacitor is now 50 V rated. The TVS's specified pulse clamp is 35.5 V; actual overshoot, pulse energy and wiring inductance need measurement. It is not a regulator for sustained overvoltage. The 5–22 V target is measured at the board connector, including source tolerance and cable drop. A nominal 24 V supply is unsuitable.

A LMR51420 buck produces approximately 3.97 V, then a TPS613222A boost produces 5 V. This supports input below the voltage required by a simple 5 V buck. Both converters use small leaded SOT packages, with external inductors and an SMA Schottky rectifier. The LM339LV input comparator runs from regulated 3.3 V; the sensor's 0–30 V signal is divided before reaching it. Do not substitute a 30 V signal for a 30 V comparator supply rating.

Per-port voltage selection is physical. Software can enable/disable the two rails, report their voltages, and latch faults. Safe software selection between 5 V and a 22 V rail would need additional reverse-blocking switches and interlocks on every port. The shunt solution reduces parts, heat and assembly complexity. A separate regulated 12 V output would add another converter, so this revision offers regulated 5 V or switched battery voltage.

The software protections supplement hardware current limiting. Sensor voltage health uses ADC measurements with startup blanking. The battery switch's open-load diagnostic may assert at light load even when voltage is healthy; firmware uses voltage to distinguish that condition. Fault clear is an explicit recovery action. Persistent settings can automatically enable requested rails after a normal reboot, while watchdog/fault handling forces them off as described in the firmware documentation.

The design uses 0805 or larger passives, SOIC logic/transceiver packages, LQFP32 MCU, through-hole terminals and 2 mm selector headers. U3 has an exposed pad that needs paste and heatbed/reflow; soldering only its visible leads is insufficient. Through vias close to IC lead escapes require reliable solder mask/tenting and visual inspection. The preferred prototype process is a 0.10 mm stencil, controlled paste/reflow appropriate to the actual alloy, followed by hand soldering of terminals and headers.

The deterministic checks publish their assumptions in `reports/electrical/calculations.json`. They budget both sensor rails at 300 mA, additional bias current, a generous 150 mA logic allocation, 80% efficiency per converter, and hot input-path resistance allowances. These are design estimates, not measured temperature or output-current guarantees. Comparator bias/leakage and boost ripple bounds include engineering allowances where the datasheets provide only typical values.

The TI boost model was exercised in ngspice with PSpice compatibility, `SS=0` for startup, and a syntax-only initial-condition adaptation. The original vendor file is retained. Both completed startup simulations settle near 4.99 V: approximately 420 mA with a 3.97 V source and 462 mA with a 3.80 V source. The latter has about 3.1 mV simulated steady-state ripple and 0.753 A peak inductor current. The upstream buck is represented by a finite-resistance source; the model does not establish cascaded-loop stability, temperature behavior, quiescent current or actual PCB parasitics. The sampled waveform, plot, model adaptation and measurements are included; these are model results, not hardware measurements.

Prototype qualification must cover startup at 5 V and 22 V, simultaneous rail loading, sensor inrush, brownout recovery, short circuits, rail backfeed, CAN error recovery, comparator thresholds/leakage over temperature, crystal startup/drive, power ripple, converter temperatures, wire retention and pogo alignment. Native ERC/DRC and Gerber connectivity checks cannot replace these measurements.

The routed-copper audit estimates 4.749–4.760 V at a port carrying the full 300 mA 5 V load, using the converter minimum, hot switch resistance and additional connector/shunt/ground allowance. This is a calculated budget, not a measured output guarantee. Input trace losses prompted increasing the total trace/connector/return allowance to 110 mΩ in the low-input calculation. The minimum practical sensor voltage and simultaneous-load rating still require bench verification.

The ground-return review uses two DC resistor-sheet meshes of the exported Gerbers and correlated component tolerance corners. A dedicated return trace and three additional stitches improve the sensor ground return and preserve the logic thresholds under 108 combined-load/supply cases. These calculations include a 15% ground-potential inflation allowance; they do not model switching-frequency ground bounce. The final programming escape uses straight outward contact traces, a top pour exclusion and a native 0.508 mm contact-to-foreign-track rule, checked against the TC2050 drawing.
